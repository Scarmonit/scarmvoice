An audit pass, and a switch that had never once worked

**Hardware acceleration could not be turned off**

*Settings → System* has an *Enable Hardware Acceleration* switch, and it offers to
restart the app because the setting can only take effect at launch. Turning it
off saved, read back as off, and restarted — and did nothing at all, on that
launch or any launch after it. The setting was read a moment before the thing
that stores it had finished loading, so the answer was always "no preference".

Anyone who turned it off to get away from a graphics driver problem — a black
window, flickering, a crash on start — got no relief no matter how many times
they tried.

It works now, and the restart it offers no longer throws the setting away on the
way out: restarting in place used to stop the app dead rather than close it, so
the change you had just made was still waiting to be written when the process
ended. That also left you in the member list for a while after you had gone, and
handed the keyboard hook to the new copy while the old one still held it.

**One message could stop a whole channel from drawing**

A single line with enough formatting in it — many thousands of `*emphasis*`
marks on one line, well within the length the server accepts — broke the message
list outright. Not just that message: everything after it stopped appearing too,
and it stayed broken on every refresh until the message scrolled out of range.
Anybody could have done it to any channel, by accident or otherwise.

**Typing `constructor:` into search killed the search box**

The search box understands `from:`, `has:`, `before:` and so on. Two words that
are not operators at all — `constructor:` and `has:constructor` — were treated
as though they were. The first stopped the box dead: no results, no dropdown, no
response to further typing until that word was deleted. The second emptied the
message list and said nothing matched.

**Signing out left the previous message in the composer**

Everything else typed into the app is cleared when a session ends, deliberately,
so the next person to sign in on the same machine does not find it. The message
box actually on screen was not — so its text, any files staged beside it and the
reply it was attached to all survived into the next account, where a single
press of Enter would have sent them under that account's name.

**Push-to-talk opened the microphone anyway**

Joining a call on push-to-talk is supposed to leave the microphone shut until
you press the key. If you had also chosen a specific microphone in Settings, it
was opened at the moment you joined and held for the whole call — so Windows
showed the microphone as in use from the moment you joined, whether or not you
ever said anything. Choosing a microphone also made the app close and reopen it
on every single call, needlessly.

**Smaller ones**

- **Notification level looked like it refused your click.** Choosing *Only
  @mentions* for a channel put the tick straight back on *All Messages*. The
  choice was saved; the control just repainted itself from the old answer, so it
  took two clicks to look like one.
- **A keyboard shortcut with Ctrl, Alt or Shift in it could not be recorded.**
  The recorder stopped at the first key, which for any combination is the
  modifier — so Ctrl+Shift+M was stored as "Ctrl". The hint that suggests using
  a modifier was advice the recorder would not take. A single key on its own
  still works exactly as before.
- **Enter did nothing in the conversation finder.** Highlighting somebody in
  *Find or start a conversation* and pressing Enter — which the footer offers —
  never opened the conversation, however many times it was pressed.
- **The Threads panel could be out of date.** Starting a thread and opening the
  panel could show a list without it, confidently and with no sign anything was
  missing.
- **`has:embed` was quietly rewritten.** Opening *More filters* on a search
  containing it showed "Links" instead of "Embeds", and pressing Apply changed
  what you had typed.
- **Clear in More filters did not clear the date.** A date you had wiped came
  back on Apply if you added a date row again without filling it in.
- **A deafened person showed no headset icon** unless you were in the call with
  them, though a muted one always showed the microphone icon.
- **A second update in one sitting opened at "100% downloaded"** with a full bar
  before it had fetched anything.
- **Three things drawn from an old copy of their own rule:** the conversation
  finder went darker and dimmer under the pointer instead of lifting, the update
  notice's icon was undersized, and the *Mute for me* toggle stopped its handle
  short of the end, so a switch that was fully on read as half thrown.

**Also**

- Every fix above is now pinned by a test, including the message-list failure
  and the hardware-acceleration switch — that one checked against the real
  application rather than a stand-in, since it is a question of what happens
  before the app has started.
- One test in the suite passed or failed depending on how busy the machine was.
  It waited a counted number of turns for something on a real timer; it waits
  for the timer now.
- These notes themselves read correctly in the app for the first time. A bullet
  written across more than one line — which is most of them — was cut off at the
  line break, and the rest of the sentence appeared underneath as a stray
  fragment. Every release published so far looks that way in *Settings → About*;
  from here they do not.
