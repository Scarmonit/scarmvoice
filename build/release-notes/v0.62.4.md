The settings screen, measured against the real thing

**It was 40% too short**

- The window was a 760px box floating in the middle of the screen with about 320px of empty space above and below it. It now leaves 72px top and bottom, like the real thing does — so the preview, all of Text Readability and most of Visual Density are visible at once instead of everything needing a scroll.

**Sliders show how far along they are**

- The biggest functional miss on the Accessibility page: the track was the same flat grey on both sides of the handle, so the only way to read a value was to look at where the handle sat. The part you have travelled is filled in now.

**Controls that were the wrong weight**

- **Radio buttons** were a thin ring with a dot floating inside it, in the same colour as the ring — almost no contrast. A selected one is a solid filled circle with a white dot in the middle now.
- **Switches** in their off position were a filled mid-grey pill, which reads like a third state rather than "off". Off is a dark well with a thin outline now, and the switch is a little wider.
- **The search box** in the settings rail had no border at all. It has one, like every other field in the app.
- **Section headings** — "Text Readability", "Visual Density" — were about 13% too small.
- **Your profile picture** at the top of the rail was about 30% too small. The two lines of text beside it were already right.
- **The active tab pill** sits a little further in from the edge of the rail, and its fill is two points lighter.

**The preview pane**

- It was a filled card containing a header strip, a divider and a nested darker panel — three surfaces where there should be one. It is a single outlined box now, and the word "Preview" above it is a quiet caption rather than a white heading.
- Two things in it were wrong about your own app, not about the styling:
  - It drew a **generated initials circle** instead of your actual profile picture, while the message list, the sidebar and the profile header a few inches away all showed the real photo. It uses your real avatar and your real name now.
  - Its two **reaction pills** looked identical, so it could not show the difference between a reaction you are part of and one you are not. The first one is now drawn in the reacted state, which is the point of previewing how messages look.
