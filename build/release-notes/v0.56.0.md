The window that ignored the tray

Thirty-one things that were quietly wrong, found by reading the whole app again. Several of them had been shipping for a while.

**Your window**

- "Start minimized to the tray" was ignored if you had left the app maximized. Windows would start it for you at sign-in and a full-screen, blank ScarmVoice appeared across your desktop instead of a tray icon. It stays in the tray now — and comes back maximized the first time you actually open it.
- While it sat in the tray, the app believed it was on screen. Everything meant to go quiet while you are not looking stayed awake instead.
- Launching the app a second time while an update was installing built a whole second session for the moment before the installer replaced it.

**Files, video and voice messages**

- Video and audio attachments could not be scrubbed. You could press play, and that was all — reaching the end of a long voice message or a screen recording meant watching the whole thing. You can drag the scrubber now.
- A voice message or a video could destroy its own upload progress bar part-way through sending. If that upload then failed, the failure had nowhere to appear: the file simply vanished with no error anywhere.
- A file sent in a direct message with no caption arrived as an empty message — a name, a time and nothing else — until the conversation next refreshed.
- An upload interrupted by a dropped connection reported a raw internal error and gave up for good, instead of saying the server was unreachable.

**Messages**

- A message typed while offline gave up after about thirty seconds and asked you to send it again by hand. That is the opposite of what the queue is for. It now waits as long as the outage lasts.
- A server hiccup while sending was reported as "the server refused this message", leaving it stuck behind a Retry button it never needed.
- Opening a second thread threw away the reply you had started in the first one.
- Escape closed the thread or conversation *behind* whatever was open on top of it, instead of closing the thing you were looking at.
- Ctrl+F broke straight out of Settings and any other panel, dropping your cursor into a search box hidden behind it — every keystroke then filtered a list you could not see, with no way back.
- The pinned panel kept showing the previous channel's pins under the previous channel's name, and clicking one dragged you back to the channel you had just left.
- A thread that failed to load sat on "Loading thread…" for the rest of the session. It now says so, with a Retry.
- "New message" and "Add people" did nothing at all for a moment after you clicked them, and clicking again wiped whoever you had already picked.

**Voice**

- If your connection dropped and came back during a call, everyone else watched you flicker in and out of the call for as long as it lasted.
- A screen share that failed to start — a window you had picked that has since closed, for instance — gave you no picture, no error and no explanation. It says so now.
- The connection panel claimed your call was "encrypted peer-to-peer". It never is: the call goes through the voice server. It says what it actually does.
- Soundboard clips reach other people by being mixed into your microphone, so while you are muted, or in push-to-talk with the key up, they reach nobody — but you still heard them yourself, which made that impossible to tell apart from a clip everyone heard. It now tells you.
- Join and leave chimes always came out of your default speakers, ignoring the speaker you had chosen — so the call was in your headphones and everyone's arrivals were on your desk.
- Settings said push-to-talk "works system-wide" even on machines where it had quietly fallen back to a press-once-to-talk key taken from every other application. It now says which one you have.
- Changing "Input mode" left the Push to Talk switch above it drawn the wrong way round, so the next click on it appeared to do the opposite of what it read.
- Hovering the voice button and then not joining left an audio device open for the rest of the session.

**People**

- Anyone in the Offline list showed a green "online" dot when you clicked them, offered a Block button that silently did nothing, and needed two clicks to switch from one offline person to another.

**Behind the scenes**

The board was doing database schema work in front of every single request — most of all on the voice heartbeat, five seconds apart for everybody in a call — which is the exact thing that took the whole board down once before. That is gone. Deleting a direct message left its attachment stranded on the server forever. Anyone could make "somebody else is typing…" appear for the room. And one bad moment writing to the log file used to switch off logging for the rest of the session, in the one file anyone reporting a problem is asked for.
