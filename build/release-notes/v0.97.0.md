Up-arrow to edit what you just said, and a sent DM you can actually see

**The message box**

- **Press ↑ with the box empty to edit the last thing you said.** It opens in place, ready to fix; Enter saves it and Escape leaves it alone. The fastest way to correct the typo you spotted a second too late.
- **↑ again goes back one more of yours, ↓ comes forward again.** Other people's messages are stepped over, so it always lands on something you can edit, and it stops at the oldest one on screen rather than looping round. Carry on past the newest and the keyboard is handed back to the box, ready for a new message.
- It works the same in a conversation as in a channel — in a conversation it walks your direct messages, not whatever channel is behind it.
- **With anything typed in the box, ↑ is still the arrow key.** It only ever means "edit" when there is nothing to move the cursor through, and the @mention list keeps it while that is open. The same goes inside the editor: ↑ moves through a multi-line message first, and once you have started rewriting one it will not walk away from your work.

**Direct messages**

- **A direct message you send now appears straight away.** It was going out, and everyone else was seeing it — but your own conversation was often not looking at the end of itself, so the message landed below the bottom of the screen and the only way to find it was to click into a channel and back. Sending now always brings the conversation to your message.
- **The conversation stops sliding away from the newest message on its own.** A picture or a link preview finishing after the fact used to grow the conversation underneath you, quietly leaving you short of the end without having touched the wheel — and from then on every refresh dropped you at the top of the history.
- **And it keeps your place when you have scrolled back on purpose.** Reading through older messages while new ones arrive no longer throws you to the top of what is loaded.
