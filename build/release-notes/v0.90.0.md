One gradient field — the panels stop diluting your colours

**The gradient passes through everything at full strength now**

The remaining washout had a structural cause: the app's surfaces are
stacked — a backdrop behind every column, the column itself, then panels
like the composer and the user dock on top — and the gradient was crossing
every one of those translucent layers, losing colour at each. The composer
sat behind three layers and kept barely a fifth of the gradient's
saturation.

The theme is one field now, the way the reference draws it. The gradient
crosses exactly one sheet of glass anywhere in the window: the backdrop
steps aside while a custom theme is active, and the raised surfaces — the
composer, the user dock, panels and wells — express their elevation as a
faint lightness shift instead of their own layer of paint. Raised things
still read raised; they just stop taxing the colours beneath them. The
glass itself is also thinner, calibrated against the reference's measured
surfaces.

**The window controls join the theme**

The strip behind minimize / maximize / close was flat grey while the rest
of the title bar wore your colours. That strip is painted by Windows, not
the page — the app now hands it the exact colour your gradient has at that
corner of the window, so the title bar reads as one continuous surface
edge to edge.

**In short**

- The gradient crosses one translucent layer, not two or three stacked ones
- Composer, user dock and panels are lightness shifts — no more dilution
- The native window-controls strip is tinted to match its corner
- Glass thickness recalibrated against the reference's measurements
