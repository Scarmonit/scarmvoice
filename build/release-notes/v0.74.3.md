Five quiet ones, mostly around turning the colour down

**Turning the colour down moved four panels out of place**

The account panel over your name, the two behind the small carets beside it, and
the connection details over the voice panel are all placed against the edge of
the window. With *Accessibility → Saturation* set below 100% they were drawn
about thirty pixels too low — and all four open upward from the bottom bar, so
each one came down over the button that had just opened it. With an update
notice on screen it was further out again.

They are placed against the window itself now, whatever the slider says.

**Turning the colour down left three things at full colour**

*Accessibility → Saturation* dims the whole window. Three surfaces were never
included, and they are the ones carrying most of the colour there is:

- The title bar, which is on screen at all times and holds the connection dot —
  green, amber or red.
- The update notice, which is deliberately the brightest thing in the window
  when it has something to offer. It also sprang back to full colour whenever
  the pointer was over it.
- The panel that opens when you click somebody in the member list or the voice
  channel: their status dot, their avatar and the red Block button.

All three follow the slider now.

**Clicking somebody offline said they were online**

That same panel drew its status dot green for everyone except people who were
idle — so somebody marked *Do Not Disturb*, or listed under *Offline*, got a
green dot in the panel while the row it had just opened from showed them red or
grey, an inch away.

The dot now says the same thing in both places.

**Three sliders in Voice & Audio were drawn slightly wrong**

Input Volume, Output Volume and the speaking-threshold slider were painted a
different way from the four in Accessibility, and it showed:

- The filled part of the track stopped up to eight pixels away from the handle
  it is supposed to end under, in one direction at the low end and the other at
  the high end.
- None of the numbers under them was ever highlighted, so the scale read as
  decoration — where the Accessibility sliders light the value you are on.

All seven are drawn the same way now.

**A profile card could survive the session it was opened in**

If your session ended while somebody's full profile was open — the card reached
from *View Profile* in a conversation — the card stayed on screen over the
sign-in form, and the keyboard stayed trapped inside it, so Tab could not reach
the password field. Reopening a profile afterwards also stopped moving the
keyboard into it for the rest of the session.

Everything else that floats over the app was already taken down when a session
ends; this was the one that had been missed. A private note left half-typed on
that card is saved on the way out rather than lost.

**Also**

- The stylesheet is now checked against the app's own markup for the colour
  ones, so a surface added later cannot quietly miss the saturation setting or
  lose one of its status colours — and the app itself is launched and measured
  for the panel positions, which is the only way to catch that kind of thing.
